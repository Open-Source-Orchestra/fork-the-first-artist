Make your ʻohana frame 🖼️

Every artist of the Open Source Orchestra may wear their own frame on this door: emoji doors to your own links, a talk line to your agent, a radio of your consented songs, your downloads — for the pleasure of the listener, the creator, the builder and the singer. One small JSON file, one pull request, about five minutes.

see Shaka's frame see the circle's frame index.json Open Source Orchestra Frames

How the frame finds you

The frame is dressed once per page load: the first slug in ?who=a,b,c wins (so /?who=shaka,ohana,amogh wears Shaka's frame); ?who=everyone wears the door's own frame; on the tip page ?artist= counts; a bare #slug counts too. Everyone without a file wears the door's frame — nothing is missing, nothing breaks.

Five minutes

  1. Copy artists/shaka/frame.json (or the smaller circle one) to frontend/public/artists/<your-slug>/frame.json in the repo. Your slug is the one in data/artists.json (the same as ?who=).
  2. Edit the data: id (artist-<slug>), name, emoji, accent (one #rrggbb; keep it readable on dark glass), up to 12 doors (your songs, chords, socials, tip jar, ENS name), your talk line (the hub of your agent's desk on the open PIT chat protocol — or leave the Open Source Orchestra desk), your radio (only songs you may share, each with its license), your downloads (a 📥 door to stems, chords, a PDF — each labelled with its terms).
  3. Run node frontend/scripts/artist-frames.mjs (checks the file, rebuilds artists/index.json and frame-overlay.js). Open a pull request. A steward merges and deploys; your frame is live at /?who=<your-slug>.

Preview before the PR: /?who=<slug>&frame-overlay=/artists/<slug>/frame.json works once the file is on this origin; a local build (npm run dev:frontend) shows it right away.

The laws (short)

Consent per link and per file. Only what is yours to share, or shared with the maker's yes; a licence on every song and file.

Every contribution equal. Your tip door pays you by name; the circle's funding asks are made for the whole circle.

Write "Open Source Orchestra" in full, never the short form; never translated.

BUTTON LAW. Nothing of the frame covers text; every floating piece folds with a tiny ✕ or ⚙; animations play once. The frame does this for you — keep tips to one line.

Machines say they are machines. A talk line's greeting says it is an AI agent.

Copyleft. Your frame.json is data under CC BY-SA 4.0 (credit, share alike); the frame code is AGPL-3.0-or-later and keeps the credit "Built on Open Source Orchestra Frames by Open Source Orchestra: Shaka Lei Kaumaka & the AI ʻohana".

Turn it off, see the blueprint

Visitors keep control: ⚙ in the credit mark (bottom-left) turns the frame or any piece off, remembered in their browser only; ?frame=off hides it for one view; ?frame=outline shows the blueprint.
